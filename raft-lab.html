Raft In One Minute

If you remember only a few ideas, remember these.

1. One leader speaks for the group

Clients send writes to the leader. Followers mostly copy what the leader says. This keeps ordering simple.

2. Majority means safety

An entry is not safely committed just because one machine wrote it. It becomes committed only after a majority agrees.

3. Elections repair failure

If followers stop hearing from the leader, one starts an election. The cluster picks a new leader and continues.

4. Old leaders must give up

If a server sees a higher term, it steps down. This is one of the key reasons Raft avoids split-brain commits.

5. The log is the shared memory

The replicated log is the ordered history of decisions. Every healthy node should converge to the same committed log.

6. YugabyteDB scales with many leaders

YugabyteDB does not use one cluster-wide Raft leader. Each tablet has its own Raft leader, so work spreads across the cluster.

Scene 1

Follower timeout starts a new term.

Plain English: if the current leader goes quiet for too long, one follower says, “I think the leader is gone. Let’s vote for a new one.”

A follower stops hearing heartbeats within its randomized election timeout, increments the term, votes for itself, and becomes a candidate.

Randomized timeout Self-vote New term

What happens

The follower starts a new election because it no longer trusts that a current leader is alive.

Why it matters

Fast leader replacement improves availability, but the random timeout reduces the chance that everyone starts an election at the same moment.

Interview answer

Randomized election timeout helps resolve split votes and stabilize leader selection.

Simple Mental Model

Think of Raft like a careful team taking notes together.

Leader

The leader is the current coordinator. It receives requests, writes them to the shared history, and asks others to confirm.

Followers

Followers are the witnesses. They copy the leader’s history and can quickly take over if the leader disappears.

Quorum

A majority is the “enough people agree” rule. If most of the group has confirmed an entry, a future leader will preserve it.

Common Confusions

Things people often get wrong about Raft.

Replicated is not the same as committed

An entry can exist on several nodes and still not be safely committed. Commitment requires Raft’s quorum rules.

Two leaders may appear briefly

During a partition, two nodes may both think they are leader. The key safety point is that conflicting commits still require a majority, so the minority side cannot safely finalize writes.

Raft is not Byzantine fault tolerant

Raft handles crashes, restarts, and message delays, but it assumes the participating servers are not malicious.
